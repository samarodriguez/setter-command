How to set it up

The sheet credentials live on the server, not in this page, so nobody can read your customer list by viewing source. Three environment variables do it.

  1. Go to sheets.new and name it "Tidal Leads".
  2. Extensions → Apps Script. Delete what's there and paste in docs/tidal-crm-backend.gs from the project.
  3. Change TOKEN to any random string and put your email in NOTIFY_EMAIL.
  4. Save, then Deploy → New deployment → Web app. Execute as Me, access Anyone. Copy the URL.
  5. In Vercel → Settings → Environment Variables add three:
    SHEETS_WEBAPP_URL = that URL
    SHEETS_TOKEN = the same TOKEN
    CRM_PASSCODE = a passcode you invent, for this page
  6. Redeploy (env vars only take effect on a new deployment), then enter the passcode above.

What this gets you. Every request from the homeowner form lands in your sheet and emails you straight away. During office hours the subject says call now; after hours it says which morning to call, and an optional 9am trigger sends a digest of anything still waiting.

Where your data lives. In your own Google Sheet. Not with me, not on Vercel beyond passing through. This page holds nothing once it's closed.

Why there's a passcode. Anyone can submit the homeowner form — that's the point of it. But the lead list has names, phone numbers and home addresses in it, so reading requires the passcode. Without CRM_PASSCODE set, reading is switched off entirely.

Running this without a server?

If you ever open these files straight off a laptop instead of the deployed site, there's no /api/leads to talk to. Fill these two in and the page talks to Apps Script directly — less safe, because the token is then in the page, so only do it on your own device.

Follow-up cadence in use

The NOW list is driven by this, counted from your last touch: 2 hours → day 1 → day 3 → day 7 → day 14 → day 30 → day 90. Every time you log a touch the clock resets to the next step out.

It's RepCard's published cadence, and the reasoning behind the front end of it is sound: response speed is the highest-leverage follow-up variable there is. The specific "7× more likely" figure that gets quoted comes from a study of inbound B2B web leads, not knocked doors — directionally right, don't treat the number as yours.

The one metric worth watching: what share of new leads you touch within 24 hours. Aim for 60–80%.
